01 / Requirement

Clear the cash
the bots can't

Fresenius receives thousands of payments a day across regions that must be applied to the right SAP invoices. Bots handle most of it - the workbench exists for everything else.

"The bots clear the easy ninety percent. The last ten percent is where the money - and the mistakes - actually live."- Cash Application Lead, FME

A payment arrives; a bot tries to match it to SAP. When it can't - an overpayment, a duplicate, an unreadable file - the item stalls, and an analyst reconstructs the picture by hand.

The brief: one workbench that surfaces every exception with context, recommends the action, and posts the decision back.

01.See every open item

One queue of everything the bots couldn't clear, aged and filterable by region, exception, amount and assignee.

02.Give the analyst context

Invoices, bank transaction, remittance advice, RPA input and history on one screen - no hopping between systems.

03.Recommend the next action

Post to RPA, post manually, or post on account - the app suggests, the analyst confirms, and the decision is logged.

02 / Research

Where the bots
hand off to humans

I mapped exactly which exceptions fall out of automation, and what an analyst needs to resolve each one with confidence.

3

Regions in one queue

EMEA, NA and Latam items, each with their own company codes and rules.

5+

Exception types

Overpayment, on-account, duplicate, unsupported file, unsupported country, bot failures.

5

Context sources

Invoices, bank transaction, remittance advice, RPA input and decision history.

Analysts weren't slow because the work was hard - the evidence was scattered. One exception meant SAP, the bank file, the remittance email and the bot log, in four windows.

So the design pulls all of it onto the item, pairs it with a recommendation - one screen, one post.

Aging is urgency

Items older than 24 and 48 hours are the ones that hurt - the workbench leads with them.

Context beats lookups

Every source the analyst would open lives on the item as a tab, not a separate system.

Recommend, don't decide

The app proposes post-to-RPA, manual or on-account; the analyst always makes the final call.

Every decision is auditable

Who decided, what they chose and when - captured in an exception history for finance and compliance.

04 / Product Tour

A walk through
the workbench

The screens that take an exception from a row in a queue to a posted, audited decision - plus the automated and remittance views around them.

The core flow

Queue, review, recommend, post

Six screens do the work: the open-items queue, the item review, the invoice mapping, the remittance email, the auto-posted view, and the audit history.

Workbench

Every open item, aged and filtered

Leads with what's at risk - items older than 24 and 48 hours - over a queue sliceable by region, exception, amount and assignee.

  • Aging KPIs surface the items that hurt
  • A dozen filters plus search across the queue
  • Matching source and assignee on every row
Item details

The whole case, on one screen

Every context source - invoices, bank, remittance, RPA input, history - as tabs beside an AI-recommended action.

  • Invoices scored against SAP with reason codes
  • Recommended action: post to RPA, manual, or on account
  • Totals and residual balance reconciled in view
Invoice mapping

Map the payment to the right invoices

A searchable list of SAP invoices to select against the payment - count and total kept live so it reconciles before posting.

  • Search and filter SAP invoices by date and amount
  • Multi-select with a running total of what's mapped
  • Reconciles the payment before it posts
Remittance email

Turn an email into a matched payment

The original email and attachment beside a "map to payment" control - link the advice to the right check, then archive.

  • Original email and PDF attachment in view
  • Map to check number and document number
  • Archive once the advice is applied
Auto posted

What the bots cleared, on the record

Everything the bots matched cleanly, read-only - confirm what never needed a human at all.

  • The successful bot matches, visible and searchable
  • Same filters as the workbench for continuity
  • Proof of how much automation is carrying
Exception history

Every decision, auditable

The audit trail: who decided, what they chose, the clearing document and the timestamp - filterable for compliance.

  • Decision maker, choice and timestamp captured
  • Clearing document number ties back to SAP
  • Filter by status, recommendation and bot error
Supporting screens

The rest of the app

Sign-in, the aging and threshold views, the item history, the AI remit extract, and the post confirmation.

Design system

Components & states

The reusable pieces behind the workbench - the top navigation and its states, the filter popovers that slice the queue, and the in-table row menu.
