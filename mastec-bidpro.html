01 / Requirement

Turn drawings
into a bid, faster

MasTec bids on large infrastructure work. Every bid starts as hundreds of pages of engineering drawings an estimator reads by hand to build a takeoff.

"The takeoff isn't hard - it's just enormous. Miss one line across a hundred pages and the whole bid is wrong."- Senior Estimator, MasTec

Every pole, transformer, conductor and labor item on a set of drawings has to end up as a line in the estimate - categorised, counted and located. Done by hand, a single bid could take an estimator days of markup before pricing even begins.

The brief: let AI do the reading and the counting, but keep the estimator the one who reviews and approves - so speed never costs trust in the number.

01.One queue for every bid

Project, status, documents and last edit on a single board - no bid lost between an inbox and a shared drive.

02.AI does the takeoff

Upload the drawings and the engine extracts each item - category, work type, item and location - into a structured table.

03.Review before it counts

Every extracted line sits beside the source drawing; nothing is final until an estimator has approved the file.

02 / Research

Watching a takeoff
get built by hand

Before designing the AI, I sat with estimators to learn exactly what they read, count and second-guess.

9

Estimator sessions

Shadowing takeoffs across utility and infrastructure bids, page by page.

100s

Pages per bid

Drawing sets an estimator marks up before a single item is priced.

4

Takeoff fields

Category, work type, item and location - the shape every line has to take.

The insight wasn't that estimators wanted the AI to decide - it was that they'd only trust it if they could check it. Every line had to be traceable back to the exact drawing it came from.

So the product isn't an auto-bidder. It's a review surface: the machine proposes the takeoff, the estimator confirms it, and approval is an explicit, per-file act.

Trust needs a source

An extracted line only earns confidence when it sits next to the drawing it was read from.

Structure over prose

Category, work, item and location - a fixed schema an estimator can scan, not a paragraph to interpret.

Status is the story

Uploaded, processing, ready to review, approved - a bid's state should be legible at a glance.

Iterations are normal

Drawings get revised; re-running a takeoff and tracking versions had to be a first-class action.

04 / Product Tour

A walk through
BidPro

The screens that carry a bid from an empty upload to a reviewed, approved takeoff - plus the access controls around them.

The core flow

Upload, extract, review, approve

Six screens do the work: the queue, the create-bid upload, the extraction, the split review, the approval, and the access layer.

All bids

Every bid, one queue

The home screen is a board of every bid with its status, documents, last update and editor - so an estimator always knows what's uploaded, processing, ready to review or approved.

  • Status pipeline legible at a glance
  • Last-edited-by keeps ownership clear
  • Search and status filter across the board
Create bid

Name it, drop the drawings

A bid starts with a name, a description and a drag-and-drop for the drawing set - PDF or PNG - with each uploaded file listed and removable before the bid is created.

  • Drag-and-drop, multi-file upload
  • Each file confirmed before creation
  • PDF and PNG drawings supported
Extraction

The takeoff fills in

Inside a bid, the files sit on the left and the Extracted Bid Data table on the right. Process All runs the set; files move through queued and processing while the table builds line by line.

  • Process All triggers the AI extraction
  • Per-file status: queued, processing, ready
  • Category, work, item and location per row
Review

Every line, next to its drawing

The defining screen: the source drawing on the left, the extracted line items on the right. An estimator reads across, corrects what's off, and approves the file - the AI's proposal never becomes the bid until a person signs off.

  • Drawing viewer paired with the takeoff
  • Per-file approval keeps a human in the loop
  • Corrections happen against the source
Approval

Locked only when confirmed

Once every file is reviewed, the bid is approved through an explicit confirmation - the estimator's deliberate act - and the extracted takeoff can be downloaded for the estimate.

  • Approval is a deliberate confirm step
  • Bid status flips to approved
  • Takeoff ready to download and price
Access

Roles for a real estimating team

Around the bids sits user management: admin, engineer and estimator roles, invite and status controls, so the right people can process, review and approve.

  • Admin, engineer and estimator roles
  • Invite users and manage status
  • Last-login and activity visible
Supporting screens

The rest of BidPro

Sign-in, the pre-extraction empty state, the approval dialog, and the user-management tooling.
