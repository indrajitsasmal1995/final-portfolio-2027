Carrom Scorer

Ten of us play carrom most evenings: Mandirtala against Chatterjeehat, doubles, first side to 29. Somebody always kept score in a notes app, and by the next week nobody could agree who was winning. So I built a scoreboard we all share.

It has run every game since 8 September. The arguments now end with someone scrolling.

How it works

Recorded between boards, not after the game

  • Pick the four players on the board, tap the side that won, slide the points. That's it, and the next board starts.
  • The score, the winner and whether the game is over are always recomputed from the boards. Fix a board from last week and the game re-scores itself, even if that re-opens it.
  • A 0-point board counts as played but credits nobody. Games can end tied, and seven of ours have. Deletes are soft, so a mis-tap is never a lost evening.
  • Everyone sees the same live score on their own phone. Sign-in is a 4-digit PIN; guests can look around and save nothing.

The rule that caused the most trouble: a side that loses every board and finishes on nothing drags the target down to 28.

It's a real carrom convention, and it only applies while the losing side is still on zero. Games played before the rule existed keep their own copy of the target, so nothing in the history gets re-judged.

The table

Ranking people who play wildly different amounts

Totals rewarded whoever turned up most. Win percentage rewarded whoever played three boards on a good night. So the rating mixes three things: 40% win rate, 40% net points per board, 20% volume. 50 is average, 60+ is having a good month.

AI

The helper can't make up a number

"Ask Carromdeb" takes questions in plain English: who's in form, how a pair does together, what happens if you pocket the queen last. The rule I set myself: the model never supplies a fact. It gets our numbers, or it asks for them.

Step 1The app does the mathsBefore a question is sent, the phone builds a pack of facts, the same tables and form the screens show.
Step 2A relay holds the keyOne serverless function forwards the question to Gemini. No game logic lives there.
Step 3It can look things upFour tools let it fetch what the pack missed instead of guessing: player stats, pair stats, game lists, the rule book.
Step 4Rules come from the bookThe official laws are pulled out of a PDF into the app, so a rules answer is quoted, not recalled.

Temperature 0, a shared 200-question daily cap so a free tier stays free, and 👍/👎 on every answer. With no API key set, the app falls back to the answers it can work out itself. The AI is a layer on top, never a dependency.

Build

Decisions I'd defend

  • No build step. Plain files, deployed as written. Nothing between my editor and the phone, and it'll still run in five years.
  • Two storage backends behind one interface. Supabase when configured, localStorage when not, so it works offline on one device with zero setup.
  • Columns are feature-detected. Avatars and PINs came after launch; if a migration hasn't run, that feature quietly switches off instead of breaking the app.
  • Realtime without flicker. Updates render into a detached buffer and swap in one shot, keeping your place on the page.
  • Phone-shaped problems. Safe areas under a floating nav, a wake lock while a game is live, and a viewport height iOS reports wrongly for the first second after launch.

Still on the list: an offline service worker, and a season roll-over that hasn't yet met a real new year.
