Designing CE CoPilot and its admin console

I designed an AI troubleshooting assistant that gives field engineers a step-by-step answer they can check, and the admin console where admins track adoption, conversations, resolution rate and response time, and refine its responses.

Role
Senior UI/UX Designer
Platform
Mobile, web and admin
Status
In pilot
Year
2026
Three surfaces

One chat for the engineer, one console behind it

  • Mobile app: for the engineer standing at the tool.
  • Web app: the same chat at a desk, with the sidebar open.
  • Admin console: where admins run the pilot and support engineers turn flagged answers into fixes.
How I worked

Learned from the scope, tested with the engineers

  • Requirements came through our business analyst as the client’s scope of work, and the scope is where I learned the domain.
  • Our engineers worked alongside the client’s engineering team, and what they observed came back to me in our meetings.
  • The scope didn’t say how a flagged answer becomes a fix, so I worked that out with our own engineering team.
  • I ran the usability testing with the 100+ field engineers in the pilot myself, and their test report is where two of the results below come from.

The fix was in the documents. Finding it was the problem.

When a tool faults, a field engineer needs the right procedure, fast. It sits in a tool manual, a past service case or the system logs, and finding it meant searching all three by hand. The client also wanted every engineer to reach the same approved answer.

1,400+Approved documents to search
100+Field engineers in the pilot
Why it matters

One wrong number can break a machine worth millions.

That is why every answer shows where it came from, and safety steps always come first.

Four rules the product could not break

Everything in CE CoPilot follows from them.

01

Only approved documents

Answers come from approved technical documents and structured datasets, nothing else. Off-topic questions are declined.

02

Always show the source

Answers are built from passages found in the documents, so each one can name its sources and carry a confidence score an engineer can question.

03

Safety before steps

Lockout, venting and preconditions come before any diagnostic step, every time.

04

Every wrong answer becomes a fix

A flag from an engineer turns into a scoped, versioned fix that later answers reuse.

The opportunity was to make every answer checkable and every mistake fixable.

Read a procedure, not a paragraph

The first answers ran on as one long reply. Studying the client’s manuals, alongside a set of sample questions and the answers engineers expected, showed that breaking a reply into labelled parts lets an engineer go straight to the one they need. Every troubleshooting answer now uses the same five, in the same order.

Three questions an admin asks every week

The console is for the people behind the answers: admins who run the pilot, and support engineers who turn flagged answers into fixes. It is built around three questions.

Question 01

Is it being used?

The overview opens on activity by day, then five numbers: adoption, conversations, resolution rate, feedback coverage and response time.

Decision 01

Adoption on an average day

Not “ever used”, which only climbs. The share of engineers using it on a given day is the number that moves.

Decision 02

Every number shows its working

Each tile says how its number is worked out. A number an admin can’t check is one they won’t trust.

Question 02

Are the answers good?

Every conversation is listed with its status, confidence and sources, and opens exactly as the engineer saw it.

Decision 03

Tiles that double as filters

Clicking a count narrows the table to it, so a number and its rows are one click apart.

Decision 04

Reading a transcript leaves a trace

Opening a conversation is recorded in the audit log. These are engineers’ working chats, and looking at one should never be invisible.

Question 03

Where is the knowledge failing?

The knowledge base shows every approved document and whether it indexed. The feedback inbox is where a flagged answer starts on its way to a fix.

Decision 05

A failed file says why

“Unreadable PDF structure”, with a retry beside it, instead of a red dot. An admin can fix what they can read.

Decision 06

Revision beside every document

The wrong torque answer came from a superseded revision, so the revision sits next to every document, where an admin checks it first.

The results

Two figures come from the test report of the usability testing I ran with the 100+ field engineers in the pilot, and one from the admin console.

60%

Shorter time to find a fix

Engineers reached the right procedure faster than searching manuals.

70%

Fewer escalations

More faults solved at the tool, without calling senior support.

64%

Resolution rate

Out of every 100 chats, 64 were marked resolved, measured in the admin console.

Reflections and learnings

  • An honest confidence score earns more trust than a confident tone.
  • Correcting an answer needs the whole conversation in view, so the fix got a page of its own.
  • Designing the way back from a wrong answer mattered more than any single screen.
